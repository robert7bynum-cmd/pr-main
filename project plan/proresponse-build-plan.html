The product

Accountability, not another app

Golf clubs run on radios and memory. An issue gets called in, someone says they’ll handle it, and no record survives to say whether they did. ProResponse replaces the memory with a timestamped trail and a clock that won’t let a problem sit.

Members need no app, no account, and no phone call — a QR placard opens a form that already knows which hole they’re standing on. Staff get the report on the phone they’re already carrying. Management gets the one thing a radio never produced: evidence.

The database owns the work

A report and its queue item commit in one transaction. A webhook is only a fast path, so nothing can be silently skipped when it fails.

The model classifies, data routes

AI picks a category. An editable rules table decides who gets paged. Swapping models can never reroute a single person.

Events are the truth

Status columns serve the screen. Every metric, escalation and performance claim is computed from the event trail instead.

Architecture

How the system fits together

Three audiences on three very different devices, one database, and a work queue that survives its own plumbing failing.

Lifecycle

One report, end to end

The member waits on none of this. Triage never runs inside their request.

1
t = 0 · one transaction

Member submits

The report row and its queue item commit together. If the report exists, the work item exists — they cannot drift apart.

2
t + 0.2s

“Got it — the cart barn has been notified”

Confirmation is immediate and unconditional. The member’s job is finished here.

3
t + 1s, or within 60s if the webhook fails

Triage

A free keyword pass resolves the obvious cases. Anything unmatched goes to Claude Haiku for a category, urgency and confidence. Below 0.6 confidence it becomes needs review and goes to management rather than being guessed.

4
deterministic

Route

The rules table maps category to department to on-duty staff. If nobody is clocked in, it climbs a leadership chain and records why it skipped each level. Silence is never a valid outcome.

5
every attempt recorded

Notify

Station chime, web push, and SMS as the fallback when push fails. Retries back off; repeated failure raises an alert rather than disappearing.

6
the staff clock starts here, not at submit

Acknowledge → in progress → done

The first acknowledgement claims ownership so two people don’t drive to the same bunker. Marking done takes one tap and an optional comment. Work that genuinely can’t happen today gets scheduled instead — the honest alternative to a false resolve.

7
quiet hours excluded from the clock

Escalate, if it sits

Past the acknowledge SLA it goes to the supervisor; past the resolve SLA, to management. Nobody is paged at 4am about a bunker rake.

8
what the member sees

The loop closes

Staff choose a club-approved message for the member, separate from their candid internal note. The member’s status page updates; they never see the internal text.

Sprint · 8 days

What ships by 11 September

The near-term target is a demo that closes the deal, not an installation. A demo needs the story flawless and the edges thin — the opposite of a launch.

SMS cannot be ready. US carrier registration takes one to three weeks and can be rejected. That is a carrier process, not a schedule. Staff sign in with email magic links instead, and the registration gets filed this week so it has cleared by the real go-live.

In, and polished

  • Club-branded reporter page on a real phone
  • Triage, routing and the durable queue
  • Staff queue: claim, comment, done, schedule
  • Station chime and a demonstrable escalation
  • Six weeks of seeded history behind a GM dashboard

Deliberately cut

  • All SMS, on the carrier constraint above
  • Spanish translation of the staff app
  • Full offline sync — replaced by honest retry states
  • Watchdog and heartbeat, invisible in a demo
  • Duplicate clustering, monthly PDF, native apps

Data integrity

Knowing the work was actually done

Marked done is a claim, not a fact. Sell a GM accountability while the dashboard reports 98% resolved and members still see the same broken ball washer, and the product is worse than useless — it is lying to management.

  1. 01

    The assignee marks it done, with an optional comment. That is the whole staff interaction. Process added to the resolve step is exactly what makes a crew stop using the app.

  2. 02

    A photo is offered, never required. A picture of the repaired sprinkler is self-verifying and takes five seconds — but mandating one on every bunker rake turns the fast path into a chore.

  3. 03

    Scheduling is a legitimate answer. Without it, SLA pressure forces a choice between a false resolve and an undeserved escalation, and most people pick the false resolve.

  4. 04

    Recurrence costs staff nothing. The same problem returning to the same hole shortly after being closed is evidence the fix didn’t hold — derived automatically, with no extra tap by anyone.

Reopen rate is the number that matters to a GM. Near zero means the response-time charts can be believed. At twenty percent it means they are fiction — and that single figure is worth more than any average on the page.

Risk

What could actually go wrong

The competition is the radio

Radios are instant, free and habitual. If this becomes a second system staff update after handling something, it degrades into data entry and gets abandoned by week three. Watch for unprompted use in week three — that signal beats any feature list.

Pace of play is a trap

The loudest complaint and the weakest fit: by the time a report routes, the group has moved on. Sell maintenance, carts and facilities. Pace of play earns its keep as a pattern over time, not a live alert.

Silent triage failure

Reports stop routing, every screen looks healthy, and the club finds out from an angry member. This is why the queue and sweeper are architecture rather than a feature.

Cross-club data leakage

Unrecoverable reputationally. Row-level security ships in the same migration as each table, with an isolation test that fails the build rather than a manual check.
